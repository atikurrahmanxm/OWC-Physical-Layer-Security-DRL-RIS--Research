Fixed and Joint Optical RIS–Friendly Jamming
Control for Secure Visible Light Communication: A
Matched Training-Budget Study
Mahidul Ahasan, Atikur Rahman, Md Amit Hasan Robi,
and Md Samiur Rahman Tanim
Abstract—An optical reconﬁgurable intelligent surface (RIS)
directs reﬂected light in visible light communication (VLC),
but joint RIS and friendly-jamming (FJ) control substantially
expands the reinforcement-learning action space. We evaluate
this trade-off by extending an established DRL-FJ simulator with
a six-facet specular RIS. We compare no RIS, ﬁxed alignment
toward the legitimate receiver (Bob), and joint control over 625
jammer weight vectors and ﬁve discrete mirror conﬁgurations.
The empirical study analyzes 90 matched training runs across
three schemes, ten random seeds, and budgets of 2,000, 5,000,
and 10,000 slots, followed by 1,000-slot frozen policy evaluations.
At 10,000 slots, joint-minus-ﬁxed utility is +0.0183 (95% CI
[−0.1677, 0.2043], p = 0.8289), and mean secrecy metrics are
1.5656 bps/Hz for ﬁxed RIS and 1.4839 bps/Hz for joint control;
their paired contrast is statistically inconclusive (p = 0.4182).
Full-family 18-contrast Holm adjustments show that secondary
comparisons are sensitive to multiplicity (ﬁxed secrecy Holm-
t p = 0.0696; joint utility Holm-t p = 0.3166; ﬁxed utility
Holm sign-ﬂip p = 0.0703). Supplemental evaluations across
ﬁve geometries show that zero-shot transfer of nominal-trained
policies incurs utility gaps of 0.76 to 1.83 in the four changed
geometries relative to same-geometry oracles without retraining.
A consistent current-domain 4-PAM diagnostic conﬁrms that
positive secrecy (1.65–1.73 bits/channel use) is QoS-feasible at
20–30 dB reference SNR, whereas colocated eavesdropping yields
identically zero secrecy. The nominal comparison detects no
statistically signiﬁcant advantage from joint discrete control
within the tested budgets and delineates the limits of nominal-
geometry policies.

I. INTRODUCTION
V
ISIBLE light communication (VLC) carries data through
modulated light from lighting ﬁxtures. A receiver within
the illuminated area may also observe that signal, so a useful
indoor link does not by itself establish conﬁdentiality [1], [2].
Friendly jamming introduces controlled interference from ad-
ditional ﬁxtures to reduce the usefulness of Eve’s observation
while limiting the cost to Bob, the intended receiver. Physical-
layer security addresses this channel-level problem; it does not
replace authentication or encryption [3].
An optical reconﬁgurable intelligent surface (RIS) adds
another control variable: the direction of reﬂected light. A
mirror array changes facet orientations rather than radio-
frequency phase shifts. A facet aimed from Alice toward

Bob may increase Bob’s channel gain, but can also reﬂect
other ﬁxtures. All sources must therefore be evaluated using
the same installed facet normals, rather than separate ideal
reﬂections for each source–receiver pair.
Additional control is not automatically useful to a ﬁnite-
budget learner. Here, four jammer weights produce 625 actions.
Adding ﬁve RIS conﬁgurations increases this to 3,125. The
joint set contains the ﬁxed-alignment actions, so its best
available utility cannot be lower. A learned policy, however,
may fail to ﬁnd those actions. We ask whether joint control
improves evaluated performance over ﬁxed alignment when
feature-network dimensions and training budgets are matched.
We address this question in a stationary room using a
CNN and differentiable neural dictionary (DND) controller
adapted from the DRL-friendly-jamming framework of Liu et
al. [2]. The contribution is a documented comparison within
that implementation: a ﬁnite-aperture optical reﬂection model,
enumeration of available discrete actions, and 90 matched
training/evaluation runs. We do not claim that combining
optical RIS and DRL is new, or that this implementation
reproduces the 2024 paper’s published curves. The results
concern a speciﬁed simulator and geometry.
II. RELATED WORK AND SCOPE
Liu et al. [2] study secure VLC with multiple friendly
jammers and use convolutional features with a DND to learn
jamming decisions. Neural episodic control provides an earlier
example of an action-indexed dictionary of feature keys and
values [4]. We retain the implemented friendly-jamming rate
and BER expressions when adding reﬂected channels, rather
than substituting a Gaussian-capacity model.
Optical reﬂecting surfaces have already been studied for
VLC security. Qian et al. [5] optimize reﬂected-spot positions
using particle swarm optimization and compare the result
with a ﬁxed focus-on-Bob alignment and no IRS. Thus ﬁxed-
versus-adaptive reﬂection is itself an established comparison.
Saifaldeen et al. [6] jointly control ﬁxture weights and mirror
orientations using continuous-action DDPG with known user
positions; their experiments include mobile users and approx-
imately 100,000 training iterations. Our static discrete-action
results at 2,000–10,000 slots cannot refute those continuous-
control or mobility ﬁndings. Liu et al. [7] subsequently report
model-driven optical-RIS control for a secrecy, BER and
energy utility. The latter description is supported by its primary
abstract; the full text was not available for this review.

Our narrower comparison separates the best action available
in a ﬁnite codebook from the performance obtained by one
implemented learner. We do not reproduce those prior RIS
controllers or claim better performance than them. Their mod-

alternatives and a broader literature assessment would be
needed for a state-of-the-art claim.

V. LEARNING AND EXPERIMENTAL PROTOCOL
A. Matched controller
No RIS ﬁxes q = 0; ﬁxed RIS ﬁxes q = 1. Both learn among
625 jammer vectors. Joint RIS/FJ selects from their Cartesian
product with ﬁve conﬁgurations. Matching the CNN does not
match action-indexed DND size or occupancy; that difference
is part of the comparison.
The pre-action observation is
st = [pB,t−1, Rs,t−1, hAB(qt−1), Et−1]T .
(12)
It includes the installed channel, not the gain of a subsequently
selected conﬁguration. Eleven prior state–action pairs and the
current state form the history. Baseline and ﬁxed histories
contain 92 values; joint histories add the normalized codebook
coordinate q/2 −1 and contain 103. Both are zero-padded to
an 11 × 11 input.
The CNN uses 20 ﬁlters of size 3×3, then 40 of size 2×2,
both with unit stride, followed by dense layers 2560 →180 →
180. All schemes have 497,000 CNN parameters. An action-
speciﬁc DND uses normalized Gaussian weights exp(−∥k −
kj∥2/2); an empty dictionary returns zero. The DND update
uses Q-learning step size 0.5 and discount factor 0.5. SGD
updates the CNN using learning rate 0.01, a replay minibatch
of four, and squared temporal-difference residuals.
The ﬁrst eleven actions initialize history randomly. Later
decisions are epsilon-greedy, starting at 0.15, decaying by
0.998 per slot, with ﬂoor 0.02. A shared seed initializes
matched schemes, but their different action sets produce differ-
ent stochastic paths. Matching seeds is a blocking convention,
not identical exploratory decisions.
B. Runs and statistical unit
We analyze 90 completed protocol-v2 records: three
schemes, seeds 42–51, and budgets of 2,000, 5,000, and
10,000 slots. Each budget starts a fresh training run, not
continuation from a shorter checkpoint. Geometry, L1 cost,
CNN dimensions, and noise parameter are shared. Legacy
records with unmatched CNN dimensions are excluded.
Evaluation resets the environment and history, then uses
1,000 greedy slots with an evaluation-mode CNN and frozen
DND. No evaluation experience updates the policy. Each run
supplies one evaluation mean, giving ten seed-level replicates.
The 1,000 slots are not independent room realizations and are
not treated as the statistical sample size.
Means are reported with seed-level standard errors and
two-sided Student-t intervals. Contrasts use ten within-seed
differences and nine degrees of freedom. The study manifest
identiﬁes joint-minus-ﬁxed utility at 10,000 slots as primary;
this is not a claim of prospective public preregistration. Other
comparisons are exploratory. We retain their unadjusted values
for transparency and add a post-hoc Holm sensitivity cor-
rection across all 18 reported contrasts: three budgets, three
scheme comparisons and two metrics. We also enumerate all
1,024 sign patterns of each ten-seed difference vector. That
sign-ﬂip analysis assumes symmetry under the null; it is not a
randomized-treatment causal test. All intervals are pointwise.
These analyses describe seed variation in this protocol, not
performance across a population of rooms.

VI. RESULTS
A. Joint versus ﬁxed RIS
At 10,000 slots, utility means are 0.7056 for ﬁxed RIS and
0.7239 for joint RIS/FJ (Table I). The paired difference is
+0.0183, with 95% CI [−0.1677, 0.2043] and p = 0.8289.
The interval includes differences favoring either scheme. It
establishes neither equivalence nor the absence of a beneﬁt at
larger budgets or in other geometries.
The mean secrecy metric is 1.5656 bps/Hz for ﬁxed RIS
and 1.4839 bps/Hz for joint control. Their paired difference
is −0.0817 bps/Hz (95% CI [−0.2996, 0.1362], p = 0.4182).
The numerical ordering favors ﬁxed RIS, but the uncertainty
does not support a superiority claim. Utility and secrecy
orderings differ because of the cost term: mean amplitude costs
are 2.15 and 1.90 for ﬁxed and joint control, respectively.
B. Comparison with no RIS
At 10,000 slots, ﬁxed RIS improves utility over no RIS
by +0.2051 (95% CI [0.1220, 0.2881], unadjusted p
=
0.00034). Its secrecy improvement is +0.2451 bps/Hz (95%
CI [0.0999, 0.3902], p = 0.00409). These are improvements
within the speciﬁed metric model and geometry.
Joint control improves utility over no RIS by +0.2234
(95% CI [0.0394, 0.4074], p = 0.02261). Its secrecy dif-
ference is +0.1634 bps/Hz, but the interval includes zero
([−0.0226, 0.3494], p = 0.07818). The ﬁxed-RIS statistic
must not be used as evidence that both schemes improve
both metrics. Baseline and joint means also remain unchanged
between 2,000 and 5,000 slots; more training did not improve
every policy.
C. Multiplicity and distributional sensitivity
The ﬁxed-minus-no-RIS utility contrast has Holm-adjusted
t-test p = 0.00614 across the full 18-comparison family. The
corresponding secrecy contrast has adjusted p = 0.06956.
For joint minus no RIS, the adjusted values are 0.31661 for
utility and 1.00000 for secrecy. The ﬁxed utility contrast also
has an unadjusted sign-ﬂip p = 0.00391, which becomes
0.07031 after the same full-family correction. Consequently,
the apparent strength of secondary ﬁndings depends on the test-
ing assumptions and multiplicity treatment. Higher observed
means should not be described as universal or conﬁrmatory
security gains.

E. Recorded computational cost
Mean elapsed training times at 10,000 slots are 1,781.7 s for
no RIS, 1,608.2 s for ﬁxed RIS, and 1,632.2 s for joint control.
These are original execution measurements, not controlled
hardware benchmarks. Worker-pool execution shares machine
resources; memory occupancy and host load can affect time.
The records do not support a blanket computational-superiority
claim. Mechanical actuation time is not included.

VII. SUPPLEMENTARY GEOMETRY AND CHANNEL

A. Finite-grid sensitivity and frozen-policy transfer
The supplemental protocol ﬁxes ﬁve geometries before
evaluating their outcomes: nominal; Bob (2, 3, 0.85) and Eve
(3.5, 1.5, 0.85); swapped nominal Bob/Eve coordinates; Eve
moved to (1.9, 1.7, 0.85) near nominal Bob; and receivers
colocated at nominal Bob. We enumerate 3,125 actions in
each geometry at noise offsets of −3, 0 and +3 dB relative
to the historical numeric noise parameter. These 15 cases are
sensitivity analyses of the implemented metric, not a physical
calibration of that parameter.
We also evaluate the 30 nominal-trained 10,000-slot policies
in the four changed geometries, using 200 frozen slots per
policy and geometry. The resulting 120 cases are zero-shot
policy transfers. The mirror codebook is reconstructed from
exact new positions before evaluation, so this is not unknown-
CSI testing, learned tracking or a moving-user experiment.
Nominal results in Fig. 7 use the original 1,000-slot evaluation;
changed geometries use the separate 200-slot transfer protocol.
Means and standard errors use ten seeds, and each policy is
compared with the oracle of its own action set in the same
geometry.
Figure 7 and Table IV quantify the sensitivity of the learned
and oracle policies to geometric displacement. In the nominal
geometry, the ﬁnite-grid oracle achieves utility 0.7119 (no
RIS) and 0.9959 (both ﬁxed and joint RIS), leaving modest
gaps of 0.2113, 0.2903, and 0.2720 for the respective learned
policies. When user positions are altered without policy retrain-
ing, performance degrades sharply. In the changed-positions
geometry, all three nominal-trained policies yield negative
mean utilities (−0.6451 baseline, −0.7876 ﬁxed RIS, and
−0.5608 joint control), producing optimality gaps exceeding
1.6 against oracles that attain positive utility (0.9550–1.0418).
When receiver locations are swapped, all frozen policies suffer
complete secrecy collapse (Rs = 0.0, utility ≤−0.76),
whereas the geometry-aware oracle maintains positive utility
(0.7119–0.7719) by selecting conﬁguration q = 2 to direct
reﬂections toward Eve. In the nearby-Eve and colocated ge-
ometries, both the frozen policies and the discrete oracles yield
zero secrecy. Oracle utility is zero, while the frozen policies
retain mean utilities of −0.82 (no RIS), −0.86 (ﬁxed RIS), and
−0.76 (joint control) because they continue to incur jamming
costs. The colocated case functions as an empirical negative
control, verifying that no controller can manufacture physical-
layer secrecy when spatial channel differentiation is absent.
These outcomes conﬁrm that nominal DRL policies fail to
generalize zero-shot to altered room geometries, even when
mirror codebooks are updated with exact coordinates.

VIII. LIMITATIONS AND REPRODUCIBILITY
Learning is performed in one stationary nominal geometry
with one oracle codebook and one learner. The added sensi-
tivity and zero-shot evaluations broaden the test conditions,
but they do not supply independently trained learners in those
geometries or validate continuous mobility. Fixed q = 1 bene-
ﬁts from known geometry and happens to contain the nominal
best ﬁnite-grid actions. Earlier scenario-speciﬁc project runs
are not pooled here because their protocols and budgets differ.
Continuous-action prior controllers were not reimplemented,
so no state-of-the-art comparison is claimed.
Neither a soft reward nor a positive secrecy metric guar-
antees uninterrupted service or prevents all decoding by Eve.
The study does not transmit packets or test coding, hardware,
movement, blockage, or cryptographic security. Near-zero
modeled BER can follow from the inherited expression and
scaling; it does not demonstrate measured error-free reception.
The unresolved noise normalization and literal BER conven-
tion limit physical interpretation. The 2024 paper’s published
numerical curves have not been reproduced exactly.
The evidence includes 90 raw run records, a protocol man-
ifest and saved CNN/DND artifacts. Antigravity executed the
98-test implementation suite and a fresh reload of all 90 saved
policies. Every policy was evaluated for 1,000 slots, comparing
360,000 stored metric values across utility, secrecy, BER and
amplitude cost at absolute tolerance 10−10. The replay also

10
checks ﬁrst actions and full RIS-conﬁguration counts. These
are implementation and inference-replay checks, not a proof
of hardware validity. Training records preserve summaries and
coverage counts rather than a complete exported slot trajectory.
Run Git identiﬁers do not uniquely identify uncommitted
code used during execution. Current ﬁle hashes support later
auditing but do not retroactively prove the original training
working-tree state.
IX. CONCLUSION
Adding an optical RIS changes the friendly-jamming sim-
ulator’s channels and available decisions. In 90 architecture-
matched runs, joint control has a slightly higher utility mean
than ﬁxed RIS, but the paired interval includes zero; their
secrecy comparison is also inconclusive. Fixed RIS has higher
observed nominal utility and secrecy than no RIS, although the
strength of secondary evidence depends on distributional as-
sumptions and multiplicity correction. Enumeration separates
the best available action from the policy found by learning.
The current-domain diagnostic also separates physically con-
sistent information/error calculations from inherited reward
conventions. These distinctions are necessary before attribut-
ing a beneﬁt to joint control. Continuous mobility, uncertain
channel information and retraining under the consistent ﬁnite-
alphabet model remain outside this study.
